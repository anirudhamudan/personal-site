Turning a manual screening call into a self-serve product flow

Students used to need a sales agent's screening call before they could talk to a counsellor, the person who guides their university applications. I replaced that call with a flow where students book a counsellor themselves. 40K students a month now use it.

Before

A sales agent screened every student on grades, test scores and passports. Those who passed waited for a counsellor to call back, at no set time.

The bet

Intent matters more than eligibility: a keen student will sort out gaps like not having a passport yet. And a counsellor as the first conversation works for both sides: they judge a student better than a sales agent with a checklist, and the student gets real advice from the first call.

What I built

A shorter signup form, then a video call with a counsellor, right away or at a slot the student picked. As a product, not a phone process, we decided what every student saw and when.

Result

70% more students got a university shortlist. The rest of the funnel held, so 70% more went on to apply.

12%cheaper to get a student to book a counsellor

Leading with a university shortlist in 60 seconds

To see which universities they could apply to, students first had to book a call with a counsellor. I made that list free and instant, ready in 60 seconds, which drew in more students and cut what we spend to get each one to book a counsellor.

Before

The only next step on Leap's site was to book a counsellor. Even to see which universities they could get into, a student had to book a call and sit through it: a big ask from someone who had just arrived, and expensive to pay for in ads.

The bet

Give students something useful upfront and more of them will sign up. Once they have seen their shortlist, many will want a counsellor to help them act on it, so the same ad spend gets us more bookings.

What I built

I rebuilt the top of the funnel, from ads to landing page to signup, around one offer: your university shortlist in 60 seconds. While the shortlist loaded, a prompt showed what a counsellor adds, so students who came for the shortlist still went on to book a call.

Result

More students booked a counsellor for the same ad spend, so each booking cost 12% less.

Building an AI voice agent that talks to students

A counsellor's first call with each student went on collecting basic details. I built an AI voice agent that held that conversation on its own, from understanding a student's goals to building their university shortlist. Students rated the call 8/10.

Before

A counsellor's first call with every student went on collecting the basics: grades, test scores, budget and goals.

The bet

An AI agent could take that first call, so counsellors only talk to students who already have a shortlist.

What I built

A voice agent that talked to students, understood their goals and built them a university shortlist. I owned it end to end: the conversation, the prompt, connecting it to our systems, and the evals.

Result

Students liked it, rating the call 8/10 on average. But they still wanted a counsellor to talk it through before deciding, so the call we hoped to skip still happened. The product worked, but the case for removing that call didn't, so we kept it.

Helping students write a stronger application essay

Students used to answer essay questions over WhatsApp, often with generic AI-written text that editors had to rework. I built a guided flow that shows sample answers and asks follow-up questions to draw out real detail. 95% of students now finish it.

Before

Every university application needs a statement of purpose. Students answered questions over WhatsApp, often with generic text pasted from ChatGPT, and editors spent about an hour per essay digging for real details.

The bet

Students had the material; the process just wasn't drawing it out. Specific questions, with examples of what a good answer looks like, would work better than asking them to write better.

What I built

A guided flow in place of WhatsApp. Every question came with sample answers, so students could see the level of detail needed, and pointers on what to talk about. Once they finished, AI scored their answers and asked up to three follow-up questions, showing how much those would raise the score. I designed the product and wrote the prompts, so that long, polished answers can't score well without real details, and the AI never adds a fact the student didn't give.

Result

Editors spend about 25% less time per essay, 95% of students who start now finish, and students rate the experience 9.1/10, up from 8.0.

Headroom

Claude usage limits in your Mac menu bar.

PlatformmacOS 13+BuiltOct 2026StackSwift
Download ↗GitHub ↗

Why I built it

I use Claude for most of my work, and the only way to see how much of my limit was left was to go and look for it. Usually I found out by running out, in the middle of something.

I wanted the number to just be there, the way battery percentage is.

What it does

Usage in the menu barYour 5-hour session, e.g. 48%.
A quiet warningA dot turns yellow when close, red when out.
Every limitWhat's used, and when each one resets.

Since then

I styled it to feel like part of Claude, so it sits in the menu bar like it belongs there, not like a side hack. I've used it every day since.

What I love most: it's now faster to build a fix for your own problem than to go looking for one.
